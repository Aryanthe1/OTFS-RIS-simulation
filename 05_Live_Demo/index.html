Judge Q&A — talking points
Why OTFS only on this one link?
OTFS is used only on the roadside sensing link (RSU → RIS → reflected echo). The vehicle's own alert still travels over standard C-V2X (OFDM-based) — no OTFS receiver is required in any car.
Why MMSE, not zero-forcing?
An earlier ZF-equalized version of this exact simulation had OFDM beating OTFS at every SNR — ZF amplifies noise at channel fades, and OTFS's SFFT spreads that damage across the whole grid. Switching to MMSE (the validation roadmap's actual target) is what recovers OTFS's real advantage from ~9–18 dB.
Is the OTFS advantage unconditional?
No — the grids above are honest about that. At very high speed and fixed SNR, fractional Doppler leakage narrows OTFS's edge again. That's a real, first-hand-observed limit, not a simulation bug.
Why hybrid RIS, not fully active or fully passive?
Passive RIS suffers multiplicative path loss; fully active adds power draw at every panel, which conflicts with SENTRY's "no second powered sensor" premise. Literature shows most of the energy-efficiency gain comes from a minority of active elements — as few as 8%, SENTRY's chosen target.
Where do these numbers come from?
Sionna (NVIDIA's link-level PHY simulator), a real 3GPP TDL-A multipath channel, SENTRY's actual 28 GHz / 15 kHz / 32×32 grid numerology, and a real 128-element array-factor computation for the RIS — all run locally, not hand-tuned for the demo.
What's still unvalidated?
The 8.7 ms sense-to-act latency budget is analytical, not hardware-profiled. The RIS ray-tracing half (sionna.rt) hasn't been run. Per-intersection installed cost is still unquantified. We say so rather than paper over it.
Simulation parameters: 28 GHz carrier, 15 kHz subcarrier spacing, 32×32 resource grid, TDL-A delay spread 30 ns, QPSK, scalar MMSE equalization with perfect CSI, 128-element RIS at half-wavelength spacing.
